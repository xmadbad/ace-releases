## For creative directors with 214 open tabs

You find the perfect reference at 1 a.m. By the pitch it's "somewhere in Downloads". Ace keeps every clip in the job it belongs to, cut and ready to share.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grab** | Right-click a video in Chrome → **Ace**. YouTube, Instagram, Pinterest, Vimeo, LinkedIn, X and many more. It lands in your project, not in Downloads/final_FINAL_v3. |
| **Cut** | **I** and **O** mark the moment. The other 57 seconds can stay on the internet. |
| **Fix** | Crop to 9:16 or 4:5. Rotate the one somebody filmed sideways. Make the green screen transparent. |
| **Point** | Arrows, boxes, circles and words, right on the video. "This bit" explains itself. |
| **Ship** | GIF, WebP, MP4, WebM or ProRes. Light enough for Slack, sharp enough for the deck, and squeezed under a max size when the client's inbox is picky. |
| **Sequence** | Line up the best bits, scrub and trim them like an edit, export one file for the pitch. No editing software was harmed. |
| **Ask** | Or tell Claude what you need ("the three seconds with the dragon, as a GIF under 2 MB"). Ace does it in its own window, where you can watch. |
| **Stay sane** | One folder per job, or the studio's whole job folder, saved as you go. Ctrl+Z for second thoughts. |

## The cheat sheet

Small habits, big difference. Steal these.

**Keep the folders boring (boring is good)**
- **One project per job.** New project, give it the job's name, done. Everything for that job lives in one folder: `sources` (what you grabbed), `images` (pictures), `gif` and `webp` (your exports), `video` (MP4, WebM, ProRes), and the project file that saves itself.
- **The studio's job folder, made for you.** New project → *Full job folder*: `PI_108_SPRING_CAMPAIGN` with 01_AFX to 11_ASSETS, and Ace's part in 04_FOOTAGE\ACE. A project that turned into a job moves in later (Project → *Move into a job folder…*).
- **Downloads stays clean.** Whatever you send from the browser goes into the open project, named after what it is, not `video (37).mp4`.
- **Canva or Magnific saving somewhere else?** Project → *Watch a folder…*: what those apps save shows up in the project by itself, used right where it is.
- **Folders you already have** can become a project too (New project → *Use an existing folder…*). Ace only fills its own folders in there; your other files stay out of its list.
- **Hand-over in one move.** Zip the project folder and send it. Everything's inside, and the paths travel with it.

**Don't hoard gigabytes**
- **Keep the moment, not the movie.** Mark it with **I** and **O**: exports only carry that part.
- **GIFs sized for where they're going.** *Web* preset for Slack and decks, *Small* for email, or type a **Max size** (say 8 MB) and Ace squeezes it until it fits. Over 15 MB? "make it smaller" shows every preset with what it would weigh.
- **Ace cleans up after itself.** Preview copies of heavy videos live in Ace's own cache and go after two weeks (Help says what they take, lets you pick how long and how big, and clears them in one click); renders you never saved go after two days.
- **Done with a clip?** Right-click → *Remove*: a file Ace downloaded goes to the Recycle Bin / Trash, so the folder doesn't keep what you don't use.

**Find it again**
- **Recent projects** open in one click, exactly as you left them: every IN/OUT, crop, rotation, drawing and sound.
- **Exports come back** in the Exports list when you reopen a project. Click one to jump back to the range it came from.
- **Changed your mind?** Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) undoes edits, even sequence changes.

**Cut fast**
- **Right-click a video in Chrome → Ace.** It downloads into the project and opens.
- **I, O, Enter.** In, out, export: it renders in the background while you mark the next one. Rather line them up first? Options → *Collect exports in the queue first*, then **Ctrl+Enter** (⌘Enter) renders them all.
- **Crop (R), rotate (Shift+R), draw (D).** 9:16 for socials, the sideways phone clip fixed, an arrow on the good bit.
- **Several at once:** pick a few videos in the list and press Enter; each keeps its own IN/OUT and crop.
- **Long video?** Ctrl + wheel over the timeline (⌘ + wheel or a pinch on a Mac) zooms in, right down to single frames; the wheel moves along. Drag the edge under the viewer up for a taller timeline with bigger frames.
- **Settings in a column:** the button at the right of Export settings puts them beside the stage, one under another, as wide as you like.

**GIFs that behave**
- **C** copies the last GIF: paste it straight into Slack, Figma or Keynote. Or drag it out of Exports.
- **A GIF you downloaded as it is** isn't encoded again: it's copied, so it stays exactly the same.
- Need it for After Effects instead? **ProRes 4444** keeps transparency; **MP4** for everything else. MP4 matches the source's quality on its own, or set CBR or VBR at the bitrate you type, as in Premiere.

**Collect pictures, not tabs**
- **Right-click a picture → Ace.** It lands in `images` and is already copied, so you can paste it into the moodboard right away.
- **Pick a few** with the round box in a picture's corner, then **Copy**: they all go together.
- **Convert** a pile of pictures to JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF in one go, without a single "Save as".

**Pitch day**
- **Sequence:** drag the best clips onto the track under the timeline and export them as one file. One link in the email instead of nine.

## Who uses it how

- **Creative directors:** build the reference pile per pitch, cut the three seconds that sell the idea, send one sequence.
- **Art directors and designers:** GIFs and stills for moodboards and decks, pictures copied straight into Figma.
- **Editors and motion designers:** clean ProRes (with alpha) or MP4 references for the timeline; speed keys to test a ramp before the real edit.
- **Producers:** one folder per job, ready to zip and hand over when the project changes hands.

## Install in a minute

**Windows.** Run `Ace-Setup.exe`. Windows gets cautious ("Windows protected your PC"): click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. The installer isn't signed yet; it's shy, not shady. It asks two things, Connect Ace to Claude and Add Ace to my browser: leave both ticked.

**Mac (Apple silicon).** Open `Ace-mac.dmg` and drag Ace to Applications. The first time, macOS says no: **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**.

**Chrome, Edge or Brave.** Ace opens with its browser extension's three steps (on a Mac, after the short tour), and ticks them off as you go.

From then on Ace keeps itself up to date: a **New version** button shows up at the top right.

## Questions people ask

**Is it really free?** Yes, for personal and commercial work. What you make with it is yours.

**Does it send my work anywhere?** No. It downloads what you ask for, and looks here for new versions. If you connect it to Claude, Claude sees what it asks Ace about: names, folders, lengths and settings, frames of a video when it looks at one for you, and a measure of its sound when it listens. That's the whole guest list.

**Can Claude drive it?** Yes. Leave "Connect Ace to Claude" ticked when you install (or Help → Connect to Claude later), restart Claude Desktop or Claude Code, and ask: "download these three links and cut the three seconds that sell it, as a GIF under 2 MB". Ace does it in its own window, where you can watch. The first time, Claude shows you around in a few lines.

**Intel Mac?** Not for now: Apple silicon only.

**Something didn't download?** The failed row has a **Report** button. Send it our way.
